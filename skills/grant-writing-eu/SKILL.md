# Grant Writing — European Funding (Horizon Europe family)

## When to use
- Drafting a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellowship (MSCA-PF) application.
- Drafting a Horizon Europe research and innovation action (RIA, IA, CSA) proposal.
- Reviewing or strengthening drafts against the EU evaluation rubric.
- Adapting a Spanish/national project description to EU format.

## Process

### Phase 1 — Identify the call and its specifics

Before writing anything, extract:

| Item | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Topic ID and call text | Funding & Tenders Portal | The exact wording of the topic ID determines what the evaluators look for |
| Type of action | Topic page | RIA, IA, CSA, MSCA-PF, MSCA-DN, ERC StG/CoG/AdG/SyG, EIC Pathfinder/Transition |
| TRL range expected | Topic text | Mismatch is automatic deselection |
| Eligibility | Call text | Country requirements, mobility rules for MSCA, PI seniority for ERC |
| Page limits | Call text + template | Hard limits, especially Part B (proposal) |
| Evaluation thresholds | Work Programme | Usually 4/5 per criterion, 12/15 total |
| Specific scoring weights | Work Programme | E.g., MSCA-PF: 50% Excellence, 30% Impact, 20% Implementation |

### Phase 2 — The Excellence / Impact / Implementation triad

EU proposals are scored on three criteria. Understand each before drafting.

#### 1. Excellence (the science)
What evaluators look for:
- **Clear research objectives** with measurable outcomes.
- **Beyond state-of-the-art**: explicit positioning vs current literature.
- **Methodology**: detailed, feasible, with risk mitigation.
- **Interdisciplinarity**: mention if applicable.
- **Sex/gender dimension**: required where relevant; cite Vademecum if applicable.

Common pitfalls:
- Writing as a national grant (over-detailed methods, under-developed positioning).
- Not citing recent EU-funded projects in the area.
- Vague hypothesis or objectives.

#### 2. Impact (why it matters)
This is the section that distinguishes EU proposals from national ones. Evaluators want:
- **Pathway to impact**: a clear narrative from research output → societal/scientific impact.
- **Target groups**: who benefits, when, how.
- **Communication, dissemination, exploitation (CDE)**: separate plans.
  - **Communication**: general public, media, policy.
  - **Dissemination**: scientific community, peer-reviewed journals, conferences.
  - **Exploitation**: industry partners, commercialization, patents (if relevant).
- **Open science**: FAIR data, open access publishing, preprints, code/data sharing.
- **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)**: quantifiable targets per impact pathway.

For MSCA-PF specifically:
- **Career development plan** for the fellow: this is half of "Impact".
- **Transferable skills**: list them.
- **Two-way knowledge transfer**: between fellow and host.

#### 3. Implementation (the plan)
- **Work plan with WPs**, deliverables, milestones, GANTT chart.
- **Resources**: justification for personnel, equipment, travel.
- **Consortium description** (for collaborative actions): expertise of each partner, complementarity.
- **Risk assessment**: critical risks with likelihood/severity matrix and mitigation.
- **Project management structure**.

### Phase 3 — Mandatory cross-cutting items

EU proposals must address (somewhere in the document):

#### Gender dimension
- For research content: state whether sex/gender is relevant to the research question. If yes, integrate analysis. If no, justify briefly.
- Reference: "Gendered Innovations" (https://genderedinnovations.stanford.edu).
- For team composition: GEP (Gender Equality Plan) of the host institution is often a requirement.

#### Open science and FAIR
- Plan for open access publication (Diamond/Gold/Green).
- Data Management Plan (DMP) commitment, with first version typically due at month 6.
- FAIR principles: Findable (DOIs, metadata), Accessible (repositories, licenses), Interoperable (standards: DICOM, OMOP, BIDS for neuroimaging), Reusable (clear licenses, provenance).
- Code: open source license (MIT, GPL), GitHub/Zenodo deposit, version DOI.
- For medical imaging research specifically: state whether de-identified data will be deposited (e.g., TCIA, IDC, Zenodo for derived features).

#### Ethics and security
- Ethics self-assessment (each call has a checklist).
- For human data: IRB/ethics committee approvals, GDPR compliance (Data Protection Officer, DPIA), informed consent procedures.
- For dual-use technology or AI Act considerations (for medical AI, the AI Act and MDR overlap matters).

#### Sustainability
- "Do No Significant Harm" (DNSH) principle if applicable.
- Climate / environment relevance.

### Phase 4 — Stylistic conventions

#### Tone
- **Academic English**, less formal than Spanish system but more structured than US grants.
- Active voice preferred; "the consortium will..." rather than "it will be done by...".
- First person plural ("we") acceptable in narrative sections; avoid in standard descriptions.

#### Visual elements
- EU proposals expect figures, especially:
  - **Concept figure** in Section 1 (Excellence).
  - **Pert chart or Gantt** for the work plan.
  - **PERT chart of WP interdependencies**.
  - **Impact pathway diagram** showing research → outputs → outcomes → impact.
- Tables for: WP descriptions, deliverables, milestones, KPIs, risks.

#### Page budget (typical for MSCA-PF, Part B-1)
- Excellence: ~4-5 pages.
- Impact: ~3 pages.
- Implementation: ~2-3 pages.
- Total Part B-1: 10 pages strict.
- Part B-2 (CV, host info, ethics): no page limit but be concise.

### Phase 5 — Specific calls — quick notes

#### MSCA-PF (Postdoctoral Fellowship)
- Strict mobility rule: applicant must NOT have resided in the host country >12 months in the last 36 (Standard) or 60 (Global, Reintegration variants).
- Two-way knowledge transfer is heavily evaluated.
- Career development is essentially weighted equally to research.
- Secondments and host institution facilities matter — name them concretely.
- For your case (relocating to Italy): MSCA-PF Standard with a host in Italy is well-aligned, mobility rule satisfied if you've been in Spain.

#### Horizon Europe RIA / IA
- Larger consortia (3+ countries usually).
- TRL relevance: RIAs typically TRL 2-5, IAs TRL 5-7.
- Industry partners often required for IA.

#### ERC (StG, CoG, AdG)
- Single PI grants. **Excellence is dominant**, Impact is secondary.
- "High-risk, high-gain" framing is encouraged.
- ERC has its own template; rules differ from collaborative actions.

### Phase 6 — Self-review checklist

- [ ] Each section maps to an evaluation criterion.
- [ ] Beyond state-of-the-art positioning explicit.
- [ ] Sex/gender dimension addressed (or justified absence).
- [ ] Open science / FAIR / DMP committed to.
- [ ] CDE plan with KPIs.
- [ ] Risk register present (table format).
- [ ] Gantt chart with WPs, deliverables, milestones.
- [ ] Page limits respected.
- [ ] Ethics self-assessment completed.
- [ ] References pointing to EU-funded projects in the area where relevant.
- [ ] Career development section (for MSCA) substantive.

## Anti-patterns

| Pattern | Why it fails | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Treating Impact as an afterthought | Impact is 30-50% of the score | Develop CDE plan in detail with KPIs |
| Open science as one paragraph | Reviewers want a real plan | Concrete repositories, licenses, FAIR mapping |
| No gender dimension treatment | Mandatory cross-cutting | Address explicitly (relevant or justified absence) |
| Generic risk register ("technical challenges") | Suggests no real planning | Specific risks with likelihood/severity/mitigation |
| Citing only own group's papers | Suggests narrow positioning | Cite competing approaches and recent EU projects |
| Methods over-detailed (national style) | Wastes pages | Compress methods; expand impact and implementation |
| Vague KPIs ("publish papers") | Not measurable | Quantify: "≥3 peer-reviewed publications, ≥2 in Q1 journals, ≥1 conference per year" |

## Output format when invoked

When invoked, produce:
1. Drafted text for the section requested.
2. Comments in [brackets] flagging where to strengthen.
3. Suggestions for tables/figures to include.
4. List of mandatory cross-cutting items not yet addressed.
